Billboard · From the file cabinet

A name in the trade papers

Growing up, I knew Dad worked in the music business. I just didn’t fully appreciate how strange it was that my own father occasionally appeared in Billboard magazine.

He never talked about these features, and I actually didn’t know they existed until I was doing some research for the show. SO. WEIRD.

Gibson Guitars · The Birth of the Les Paul · p. 188

One for the guitar books. (Literally).

Dad’s name turns up somewhere else, too: beside a photograph of a 1956 Les Paul in Gibson Guitars: 100 Years of an American Icon by Walter Carter. The feature, “The Birth of the Les Paul,” is by Tom Wheeler.

See the feature and book cover · p. 188 ↗
People · June 1, 1992 · pp. 66–67

Dad in People.

In its June 1, 1992 story on Travis Tritt, People included Dad’s memories of their early work together, along with a photograph of Dad, Travis, and Atlanta Falcons coach Jerry Glanville.

05 / Objects with stories

Not just souvenirs.

Two things that used to hang in our house. Neither is there now.

Travis Tritt · A Christmas gift

Travis's missing leather suit.

For years, the Manuel of Hollywood suit Travis wore on the cover of T-R-O-U-B-L-E hung in a glass case on our studio wall. He'd given it to Dad for Christmas.

Open the story ↗
The Warner Bros. guitar · Mossman Superlative

The Bugs Bunny guitar.

Dad had Scott Baxendale build him a custom Mossman Superlative with Warner Bros. and Looney Tunes inlays. Travis played it in the “Country Club” video. At our house, it had its own temperature-controlled case.

From our family collection

A house full of music.

Guitars and instruments were part of the furniture in our house (literally, in this photo). Dad collected them, played them, and found room for them everywhere.

When I think about home, I hear those instruments as much as I picture the rooms they were in. They were such a big part of Dad’s life that they became part of mine, too. Long before I had a playlist of my own, I was growing up inside his.
